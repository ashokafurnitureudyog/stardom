# Stardom

## Product purpose

Stardom is the premium chair brand of Ashoka Furniture Udyog, a Chandigarh manufacturer that has
been building office furniture since 1996. The site is a catalogue and a credential: it shows the
range, proves the factory behind it, and gets a serious buyer to a conversation. Nothing is sold
online. Every enquiry ends in a WhatsApp message, a phone call, or a showroom visit.

## Register

brand

The public site is marketing: the design is the product. The admin dashboard behind `/admin` is the
product register, and its rules differ.

## Users

**The specifier.** An architect or interior contractor fitting out an office in north India. Arrives
from a search or a referral, wants to know whether a range covers what the floor plan needs, and
judges a manufacturer on whether the work looks made rather than sourced. Browses on a laptop,
usually with a client on the other side of the desk.

**The procurement lead.** Buying chairs for a company, comparing three vendors, and accountable for
the decision. Wants the seven series to map cleanly onto roles: who sits in a Director chair, who
sits in a Work chair. Needs an address, a phone number, and a sense of scale before making contact.

**The walk-in.** Someone who saw the Chandigarh showroom or was sent a link on WhatsApp. Mobile,
impatient, looking for a phone number and a photograph.

## Brand

Ashoka has made furniture for thirty years; Stardom is how that work is presented now. The register
is a manufacturer's confidence rather than a boutique's polish: the brand's authority comes from the
factory, the tooling, and the years, not from adjectives.

Warm near-black ground, brass as the single accent, Playfair Display for anything that carries the
name, Montserrat for everything that has to be read. The palette is set and is not up for
renegotiation per page.

Written voice: plain, specific, unhurried. Say the year, the city, the material. No superlatives the
factory cannot back.

## Product architecture

Chairs are filed under exactly seven series, finalised by the client in September 2026. Nothing
outside this list is offered:

Signature, Director, Executive, Work, Visitor, Café, Bar.

This is the spine of the catalogue. It belongs in navigation, not buried behind a filter.

## Anti-references

- Ecommerce chrome. No cart, no wishlist, no badges, no urgency. The site never pretends to
  transact.
- Startup-landing furniture: pastel gradients, floating 3D chairs, "Elevate your workspace" laid
  over a stock photo of an open-plan office.
- Serif-italic column headings over four equal link columns, which is what the footer is now.
- Tracked-out uppercase eyebrow labels above every section, which the site currently over-uses.

## Strategic principles

1. **The factory is the proof.** Photographs of the workshop, the welding, the stitching carry more
   weight than any claim about quality.
2. **The seven series are the map.** A visitor should be able to name the series that fits their
   floor before they reach a product page.
3. **Contact is content.** The address, the hours, and the phone number are what a buyer came for.
   They belong in the layout, not at the bottom of a legal strip.
4. **Restraint reads as expensive.** One accent, one gesture, a lot of air.
